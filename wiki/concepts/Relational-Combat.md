# Relational Combat

The win-frame Neo deploys when a third party threatens a relationship
he's pursuing. Distinct from [[Erotomania]] (which is yearning) and
from [[Self-Sabotage-In-Love]] (which is internal demolition):
Relational Combat is *external* — engaged against a rival, oriented
toward winning, formally adversarial. New concept added per Neo's
call from the concept-list review.

## The canonical instance

> [!quote]
> "I fought back, and I struck as hard as I can and say Fuck You.
> Turn everything in my favor, make it a war, he spreads the message,
> and I delivered the final blow. You lost. I finally for the first
> time in my life, won."
> — [[2025-01-01-Buried-Old-Stories]] (the late-2024 [[Anette]]/[[Canyon]] war)

The frame is military-narrative throughout: war, fighting back,
delivered the final blow, won. The adversary is named ("he"),
operationalized (spreads the message), and defeated. Neo registers
this as the *first* relational victory of his life. The frame did
something the prior years' withdrawals never did: it produced an
outcome Neo could call winning.

## The structure

Stages of the pattern as documented:

1. **Threat from a third party.** [[Canyon]] (Anette's prior
   attachment) interferes with Neo's pursuit. Specifics of the
   interference are not detailed in the source, but its presence is
   named ("someone tried getting in my way").
2. **Initial retreat.** Neo's first move is the standard
   [[Self-Sabotage-In-Love]] response — block button, withdrawal.
3. **The pivot point.** *"Suddenly, she tried to make her way back. I
   couldn't just walk away like I always have. I'm sick of it."* The
   ordinary withdrawal pattern is consciously broken. Something else
   has to happen.
4. **Engagement.** "I fought back, and I struck as hard as I can."
   The exact moves are not specified; the third party's
   message-spreading is countered with Neo's own.
5. **Victory framing.** *"I delivered the final blow. You lost."*
   The outcome is consolidated narratively before any reflection on
   what was actually won.

## Why it's distinct

The Relational Combat frame is the inversion of every prior
relational-failure pattern. Where [[Romantic-Obsession-Architecture]]
generates passive yearning and [[Self-Sabotage-In-Love]] generates
internal demolition, Relational Combat generates *external action
toward another person*. It is the only such pattern in the corpus
where Neo's agency is directed outward against a rival rather than
inward against his own pursuit.

The frame is also the precondition for the [[Anette]] relationship
beginning at all. Without it, Anette would have been another withdrawal
case — possibly another [[Sen]]. The combat pattern is what got the
relationship across the threshold.

## The post-victory deflation

Notable: the frame's victory is immediately followed by collapse into
a familiar register. *"with my social inexperience I kept blowing
it... I realized I'm late. Too late in all of this."* The Combat
frame got him through the door; the
[[Romantic-Obsession-Architecture]] resumed once he was inside. The
frame is a one-shot tool, not a sustainable mode.

## Possible recurrences

- **The [[2024-04-22-My-Last-Prayer-Amen]] [[Vice-Principal-Incident]]
  posture.** Adversarial register against an authority figure. Same
  combat-style framing applied to school, not a peer.
- **[[2024-02-28-Anger]] mother-rage.** Combat ideation toward the
  mother. Internal fantasy version of the same frame.
- **[[2026-04-XX-Rebellious-Teenager-Memories]] supervisor episode.**
  Neo persuades [[Dave]] to test a supervisor's rule, the rule fails,
  the rule is changed, the supervisor is fired. The combat frame
  applied to workplace rule-gaming. *"Luck is on my side."*

The pattern's frequency outside relationships suggests the
Relational Combat instance is one application of a broader combat
disposition. Worth flagging for cross-link if and when the broader
concept gets a name.

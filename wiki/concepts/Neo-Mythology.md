# Neo-Mythology

The name as self-fulfilling prophecy. Neo's account of his own
"Neo"-ness — fantastical, futuristic, beyond-this-world — as a
generative engine for his life trajectory. Distinct from
[[Self-Mythologizing]] (which is the *act* of writing one's profile);
Neo-Mythology is specifically the *etymological* fuel.

> [!quote]
> "It might also be because of my name: Neo. It's such a fantastical
> name, futuristic? who knows, but it's something beyond this world,
> and its made me think this way. pragmatic yet fantastical."
> — [[2026-03-29-Savior-Complex-Blabber]]

## The mechanism

Neo treats the name as causally implicated in his ambitions. The
fantastical scale of his life-plan ("yes ill help people, maybe even
save the world etc") is anchored, in his account, to the name's
register — *Neo* as borrowed from science fiction futurism, the
unborrowed implication of *new* and *beyond*. The name licenses
imagination as a faculty he is permitted to exercise. *"Delusion gets
you far in life"* is the corollary.

The relevant claim isn't whether the etymology is causally true. It's
that Neo *uses* it as if it were. The name becomes part of the
narrative engine that drives [[Savior-Complex]] at world-saving
scale, [[Long-Term-Plan]] at decade scale, and the
[[Self-Mythologizing]] register at sentence scale.

## Pragmatic + fantastical

The Neo-Mythology requires holding two registers simultaneously.
*"I'm not a concrete man, i live in an imaginary world yet have the
assertion to keep going and being successful in life."* The
[[Living-Contradiction]] applied to motivation: the *imaginary world*
provides scope and stakes; the *assertion* provides operational
follow-through. Either alone would fail. Neither cancels the other.

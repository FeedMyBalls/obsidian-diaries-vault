# Fragment Entries

The cluster of diary entries that trail off, stop short, or get
written in compressed registers Neo cannot sustain. Treated as a
concept because the *not-writing* is itself information.

## The members

- **[[2024-02-29-Leap]]** (65 words). Leap-day fragment. Trails off
  mid-sentence: *"i didn't really talk about what happened to the
  days leading up to valentines. It was —"*
- **[[2024-07-22-First-Day-Of-Work]]** (201 words). First-day-of-EVS
  entry, typed on phone during lunch break. Listy, present-tense,
  no narrative arc.
- **[[2025-06-21-Everythings-A-Waste]]** (177 words). Mid-summer
  post-[[Anette]] regression entry. The brevity itself is the data
  — Neo cannot sustain even the despair-essay register that 2023
  would have produced for the same content.

## What the form means

Three different conditions produce the same shape:

1. **Voice exhaustion ([[2024-02-29-Leap]]).** The 2024 winter has
   produced [[2024-02-28-Anger]]'s uncompressed mother-rage; the
   leap-day attempt to continue collapses mid-sentence. The entry's
   inability to continue is the entry.
2. **Constraint ([[2024-07-22-First-Day-Of-Work]]).** Phone, lunch
   break, ten minutes. The fragment is what fits. Not collapse —
   capacity-bounded.
3. **Register thinning ([[2025-06-21-Everythings-A-Waste]]).** Neo
   has the time and the space; what's gone is the will to argue
   himself out of the despair the way 2023 entries did. The
   architecture of sustained writing has thinned.

## Diagnostic value

When a fragment appears at a point where prior years would have
produced a long entry, the fragment is itself the year's diagnostic.
The 2025 cluster's brevity — five entries total, two under 200
words — registers Neo's voice attenuating before the 2026 reflective
forms emerge. The not-writing precedes the new-writing.
